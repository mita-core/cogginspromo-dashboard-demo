Interactive elements

  • Period in the header synchronizes the chart title and date axis, Period metrics, Order status, status share, and Action required counters. Point-in-time values remain stable by design.
  • The sales chart supports pointer hover and keyboard arrows. Its crosshair and tooltip expose the date, current value, and previous-period value for every plotted point.
  • Period metric cards show a contextual tooltip on hover or focus and open the section that owns the metric: Orders, Inventory, or Customers.
  • Order status is a fixed period summary. Its cards are information only: they do not act as filters, receive an active state, or move the page.
  • Recent activity tabs are the only order-status filters. They update the feed in place, keep the current scroll position, and show an active state. All restores the complete cross-functional list; short results include a clear end-of-results placeholder.
  • The Action required callout opens a dedicated Action center; its three task cards open detail panels on the right.
  • Order feed rows open a detail panel populated from the selected row; inventory and customer events lead to their corresponding sections.
  • Product, customer, budget, and campaign rows open the correct detail panel with the selected row’s name, value, and rank instead of repeating the first row’s data.
  • The left navigation switches sections; each follows the same pattern: summary, filter, table.
  • Theme and notes are two toggles above the demo. Notes reveal the client quotes, decision history, data caveats, and implementation rationale next to the relevant blocks; the theme control is duplicated in the demo header.
  • The icon at the bottom of the navigation collapses the sidebar to icons; clicking it again expands the sidebar.
  • Settings is a new section at the bottom of the navigation; the product currently has no system settings screen.
  • Implementation: CSS owns navigation, filters, panels, and visual states. JavaScript remembers the theme, powers chart exploration, announces period changes, and maps selected rows into their detail panels.
  • Data reliability: verified source values are shown without a marker. Derived or unsupported comparisons retain the ° marker; known source discrepancies are documented rather than silently normalized.

Why this order

  • First: the compact Action required callout keeps urgent work visible and provides one clear route to the Action center.
  • Then: the screenshot's established commercial reading order remains intact: Trends, Period metrics, then Customers, budgets, campaigns.
  • After the commercial overview: Order status and Recent activity are grouped as one full-width operational area. The summary appears immediately above the events it explains.
  • Interaction boundary: Order status stays read-only; only the tabs inside Recent activity change the feed. This prevents a summary card from being mistaken for a navigation control.
  • Depth on demand: the dashboard remains an index. Complete records live in their sections, while the Action center gives each urgent queue room to grow.
  • Stable layout: the activity feed uses a fixed-height region, so filtering does not move the page. All contains 13 mixed events; narrow filters explain when no additional activity remains.

What moves in from other screens

  • Orders status tabs with counters remain a full-width horizontal pipeline above Recent activity.
  • Inventory receipts and customer registrations return to Recent activity alongside order updates.
  • Inventory Forecasting Recommended Order Date and Predicted Out Of Stock Date power the Reorder required queue.
  • Backordered Items becomes one of the three queues in the Action center.
  • Redemption / Manage the Unclaimed and Claimed columns power the campaigns block.
  • Budgets the top 5 by spend prevents opening the section just to review five rows.
  • Export to Excel appears on all 20 screens, so the action moves into the dashboard header.

What is removed or consolidated

  • Quick Actions five buttons that duplicate the left navigation only 200px away.
  • Recently Shipped Orders duplicates the Shipped tab in the activity feed.
  • The chart color selector and eye icons on alerts are controls without a function.
  • Redemption Dashboard, Delivery Reports and CRM Activity a page with four links, a copy of Orders, and a Coming soon placeholder.
  • Eight inventory screens are consolidated into Inventory with tabs and a shared filter.
  • Store Credit and Create Credit Claims move under Budgets & Credit because they belong to the same financial workflow.

Data discrepancies on the site

  • The Orders status total is 30+70+8+261+4+8 = 381, while the All tab shows 392.
  • The dashboard shows 354 orders for July 27 to August 26, while Orders shows 392 for the same range.
  • Orders worth $0.00 are either redemptions or errors and need a data label.
  • One entry is duplicated in the activity feed (#30436499 appears twice).
  • Budgets include Amount Spent but no limit.
  • Store Credit: limits and balances are zero for almost everyone.

What the ° symbol means

  • Previous-period deltas and sparklines: period comparisons are not currently calculated.
  • The dotted previous-period line on the chart requires the same API support.
  • Age of the oldest order on hold: the Ordered Date field exists, but the value is not displayed.
  • Weeks of stock by SKU: derived from Average Sold Per Week and Quantity On Hand.
  • Dashboard backorder count: it is missing from the response and must come from Backordered Items.
  • Figures for periods other than 30 days are illustrative.
  • Inventory receipt quantity and customer registration timing in the mixed activity feed are illustrative.

Grid and charts

  • Desktop: 12 columns, 24px gutters, an 8px vertical rhythm, and 16px card padding instead of the current 24 to 32px.
  • Trends use an 8/4 split; Period metrics use four columns; customer context uses three columns; Order status and Recent activity are stacked full width.
  • The initial screen preserves the screenshot's commercial overview, with the connected operations row below it.
  • There is one full-size chart. Everything else uses a status bar, sparklines, and proportion bars in lists.
  • There are no pie charts or secondary axes; multi-series comparisons belong in Reports.
